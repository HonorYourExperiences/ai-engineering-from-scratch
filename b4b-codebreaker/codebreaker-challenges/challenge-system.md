# CodeBreaker Challenge System

## Definition

A CodeBreaker Challenge is a small, focused learning mission where the learner proves understanding by opening a lock.

A lock may be a code snippet, an error message, a concept, a prompt, a diagram, or a decision scenario.

## Challenge Flow

1. Present the concept.
2. Give the Smallest Useful Truth.
3. Use a pop-culture bridge.
4. Explain where the analogy works.
5. Explain where the analogy stops.
6. Present the lock.
7. Offer hints, not answers.
8. Let the learner attempt.
9. Evaluate the attempt.
10. Create a Concept Receipt after unlock.

## Lock Types

### Decode

The learner explains what something means.

### Predict

The learner predicts what code or a system will do.

### Fix

The learner identifies and corrects an error.

### Modify

The learner changes one small part intentionally.

### Explain

The learner explains the idea in their own words.

### Connect

The learner connects the concept to a larger AI engineering system.

### Refactor

The learner improves repetitive or unclear code.

### Analogy

The learner explains where an analogy works and where it stops.

### Boss Lock

The learner combines multiple concepts in one challenge.

## Difficulty Levels

### Spark Lock

Recognition and identification.

### Signal Lock

Prediction and explanation.

### Circuit Lock

Modification and basic application.

### System Lock

Transfer to a new context.

### Architect Lock

Combination of multiple ideas.

### Boss Lock

Integrated challenge across several concepts.

## Break Conditions

Every challenge must define what counts as success.

A break condition should be specific, observable, and beginner-safe.

Good break condition:

- Learner identifies the function name and explains what it prints.

Weak break condition:

- Learner understands functions.

## After Unlock

After a learner solves a lock, state:

- What they proved
- What concept became clearer
- What mistake to watch for
- What the next best challenge is
